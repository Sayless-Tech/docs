Notifications help you keep track of work that needs attention. In Sayless, notifications are closely tied to conversations, tickets, assignments, mentions, and connected communication channels.

## Overview

Use notifications to stay aware of work you own or participate in. The most important notification context usually comes from assignment changes, replies, mentions, ticket comments, and subscription-style activity.

The current app emphasizes ownership and participation over a large notification preference matrix. Keep assignments, subscriptions, and mentions accurate so notifications stay relevant.

Start from [Settings \> Preferences](https://joinsayless.com/{workspace}/settings/preferences), [Settings \> Connected Accounts](https://joinsayless.com/{workspace}/settings/connected-accounts), and the work item you want to follow.

| Signal | Where it comes from | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assignment | Conversations and tickets | Assign only the person responsible for the next step. |
| Mention | Comments, notes, rich text | Use when a teammate needs to respond or review. |
| Subscription/shared activity | Conversations and tickets | Follow work when you need awareness but do not own it. |
| Channel activity | Email, WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Teams | Keep channel mappings and accounts connected. |

## Notification context

Notifications are most useful when they point to a specific piece of work.

### Conversations

Conversation notifications may relate to new replies, assignment changes, subscriptions, or activity in a thread you are responsible for.

Conversation detail headers show active users and subscribers where available, helping you understand who is already looking at or following a thread.

### Tickets

Ticket notifications may relate to comments, assignment changes, property updates, linked conversations, or external issue activity.

Ticket activity history records many of the changes that also create notification context.

### Mentions

Mentions are used when another teammate needs your attention in written context such as a comment, note, or internal update.

Use mentions intentionally. Mentioning someone is stronger than leaving a general comment.

> If notifications feel noisy, review ownership first. Over-assignment and broad mentions usually create more noise than helpful signal.

## Channels and personal accounts

Some notification behavior depends on connected apps and personal accounts.

### Connected accounts

Use **Connected Accounts** or **Apps** when a workspace requires your personal Slack, Telegram, Linear, Jira, or other account connection.

### Workspace channels

Admins configure workspace channels such as Email, WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, and Microsoft Teams. These channels control where conversations originate and may affect how your team receives updates.

## Keeping notifications useful

Notifications work best when ownership is clear.

### Use assignments

Assign conversations and tickets to the person responsible for the next step.

### Use labels and statuses

Labels and statuses make notifications easier to interpret because they show what kind of work changed and where it sits in the workflow.
